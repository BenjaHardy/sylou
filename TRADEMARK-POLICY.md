## Why this policy exists

Sylou is open-source software, released under the GNU Affero General Public License v3.0. The FLOW methodology that underpins Sylou is published under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 license. Anyone can use, modify, run, and redistribute Sylou's code and methodology under those licenses.

The **Sylou name and logo, and the FLOW Pledge Badges format,** are something different. They are governed by this policy, not by the AGPL or CC BY-SA licenses.

The purpose of this trademark and the related protections is to help ensure the consistency of what Sylou stands for: the methodology, the transparency commitments, the privacy posture, so that Sylou continues to mean what it is intended to mean.

The policy is not designed to extract revenue from community use, and it is written to be welcoming to the open-source community. If the official Sylou project ever offers commercial services or licensing arrangements in the future, those will be additional offerings alongside the open-source project, not replacements for it.

If you're reading this and wondering whether your planned use is OK, the short answer is: it almost certainly is. The bias of this policy is toward permission.

## Uses that do not require permission

You may do the following without asking:

**Refer to Sylou by name** in articles, blog posts, academic papers, reviews, tutorials, comparisons, documentation, social media, presentations, and similar contexts. This includes screenshots of the Sylou interface where the name and logo appear naturally.

**Describe your work as related to Sylou** when it accurately is. For example, "compatible with Sylou," "imports Sylou data," "based on the Sylou codebase," "an alternative to Sylou," "we used Sylou for our research."

**Use Sylou to demonstrate the authenticity of your own work.** Writers, professionals, researchers, freelancers, agencies, and organizations using Sylou to demonstrate genuine human authorship of their writing, whether for clients, publishers, institutions, or their own records, are using the project exactly as intended. Display the FLOW Pledge Badge on your own work, share FLOW reports with whoever needs to see them, and reference the Sylou name to identify what tool produced the analysis. This is one of the core intended use cases for the project, and no permission is needed.

**Use Sylou in education and research.** Teachers using Sylou in coursework, students using it for assignments, researchers studying it, instructors recommending it, conference speakers presenting about it; all welcome, no permission needed. Educational and academic use is one of the core intended use cases for the project and is explicitly encouraged.

**Self-host an unmodified copy of Sylou** for your personal use, your organization's internal use, or for a community you are part of (an academic department, a non-profit, a writing group), and refer to that deployment as "Sylou", provided you make clear that your deployment is operated by you, not by the official Sylou project. For example:

- "Sylou, hosted by [Your University]"
- "Internal Sylou instance for [Your Organization]"
- "[Your Community] Sylou"

**Distribute unmodified copies of Sylou** under its AGPL license, including the original Sylou name and branding in the source code as released.

**Package Sylou for distribution** (Debian packages, Docker images, Helm charts, Nix packages, and similar). Distribution packaging is a normal and welcomed part of the open-source ecosystem.

**Contribute to the Sylou project.** Pull requests, patches, translations, bug fixes, accessibility improvements, security fixes, and operational customizations are all part of the project's normal life and need no special permission, even when distributed downstream as part of your own modified build.

**Link to the official Sylou project** at meetsylou.eu using the Sylou name and logo as link text.

## Forks and modifications

Because Sylou is open-source, anyone can fork the project and modify it. This is encouraged, that's the point of open source. The trademark question is only about *naming*, not about *modifying*.

Most forks do not require renaming. The cases where a rename is needed are specifically those where the modified version is being operated or presented as a long-term independent product under the Sylou name, in ways that could confuse users about whose methodology and commitments they're relying on.

**No rename needed for:**

- Pull requests, patches, and branches intended to feed contributions back upstream
- Bug fixes, security patches, performance improvements, and refactoring
- Language translations and accessibility improvements
- Distribution packaging (Debian, Docker, Helm, etc.)
- Local operational customizations on self-hosted deployments (custom OAuth providers, branded login pages, institutional defaults, integration with your own systems)
- Short-lived forks maintained while a PR is in review or while you're experimenting

**Rename needed for:**

- Forks operated as long-term independent projects competing for the Sylou identity, or positioning themselves as alternatives to the official Sylou project
- Modifications to the FLOW methodology, FLOW prompts, scoring rubric, pillar definitions, or Work Profile taxonomy that substantially alter the analytical output users see
- Modifications to the FLOW Pledge Badges format or the structure of FLOW reports as user-facing artifacts
- Modifications to core behaviors that define what Sylou *is*: for example, removing the transparency/text provenance tracking, or adding telemetry that violates the project's privacy commitments

The test is whether your modified version, while branded as "Sylou," could mislead users about what they're using. If yes, please rename. If no, please carry on.

A renamed fork can still acknowledge its origins ("forked from Sylou," "based on Sylou code"), as that's nominative use and is welcomed. The renaming requirement is only about the primary branding.

## Uses that need a conversation first

A few uses sit in a grey zone and are best handled with a brief email rather than left to guesswork. The threshold here is *potential user confusion about source*, not commercial activity in itself:

**Operating a paid service under the Sylou name** where users might reasonably believe they're contracting with the official Sylou project. A small institutional deployment that charges members nominal fees to recover hosting costs is different from a startup running Sylou-as-a-service at scale; the question is whether users would understand who they're contracting with. If your service is clearly branded as operated by you (not by the official Sylou project) and your users understand this, no conversation needed. If it's ambiguous, let's talk. 

**Using the Sylou name or logo in ways that imply official endorsement**; phrasing like "official Sylou partner," "certified by Sylou," or "Sylou-approved." This requires permission because the user-facing claim is one only the official Sylou project itself can make.

**Producing merchandise** (t-shirts, stickers, mugs, books) with the Sylou name or logo. Generally welcome for community/non-commercial purposes; just send a note so I know what's out there. Otherwise, do reach out.

**Registering domain names that imply official status** — `sylou-official.*`, `getsylou.*`, `realsylou.*`, and similar. Domain names that clearly indicate operator distinction (`sylou.yourcompany.com`, `acme-sylou.org`) are fine under the self-hosting permission above and don't need a conversation.

## Uses that are not OK

A small number of uses are not permitted under this policy:

**Using the Sylou name for products that compete with the official Sylou project**, whether or not they are based on Sylou's code. If you have built a human-AI writing transparency tool, please use a different name, whether you built it from scratch, forked Sylou substantively, or operate a hostile fork. The Sylou name isn't available for use on products positioned as alternatives or competitors to the official Sylou project.

**Using the Sylou name or logo to mislead users about who provides a service.** Any use that creates a likelihood of confusion about whether the official Sylou project is behind a service, product, statement, or communication is not permitted.

**Using the Sylou name or logo in connection with content that violates law, harasses individuals, promotes hatred, or is otherwise harmful.** The Sylou name is not available for use in association with such content.

**Modifying the Sylou logo.** The logo may be reproduced as-is for the purposes allowed by this policy, but please don't alter its design, colors, proportions, or wordmark. If you need a variant (monochrome, different background, etc.), just ask; variants are usually fine.

**Using the FLOW Pledge Badges format on reports generated by services not operated by the official Sylou project.** The badge is the visual signature of the official Sylou platform; using it to certify reports from third-party operations defeats its purpose as a credibility signal for users encountering a FLOW report. Self-hosters running unmodified Sylou for personal, organizational, or community use are *not* the target of this restriction (see the self-hosting section); the restriction is aimed at third-party services using the badge to present themselves to their users as if they were the official Sylou platform.

## Self-hosting: a closer look

Since self-hosting is a core intended use case, here's specific guidance:

**Unmodified Sylou, your own deployment, your own users: go ahead.** Run it under the Sylou name. The `generator_info` block on shared reports identifies your instance honestly (your domain, version, commit hash), which is enough to distinguish your deployment from the official one. Users see who's running the service and can decide accordingly. Reports generated by your instance can carry the FLOW Pledge Badge: you are using the official Sylou software and the badge represents that accurately.

**Unmodified Sylou under your own product name** (e.g., "[Your Organization] Writing Assistant"). Also fine. We just ask that you include a "Powered by Sylou" notice somewhere reasonable — a footer, an About page, your deployment's documentation — so users who want to know what's under the hood can find out.

**Substantively modified Sylou under the Sylou name.** Please rename per the forks section above. Acknowledging the origins is welcomed; carrying the Sylou identity into substantially altered work isn't.

**Commercial paid service under the Sylou name.** Send a note. As discussed in the previous section, having the Sylou name for products that compete with the official Sylou project is normally not foreseen as permitted use. 

## How to ask

For anything in the "needs a conversation" or "asking is welcome" categories, email trademark@sylou.eu.

Tell me what you'd like to do. A paragraph is fine. I usually say yes for any use that doesn't risk confusing users about the official Sylou project's relationship to your work, and I try to respond within a couple of weeks. If there are conditions worth discussing (attribution language, quality expectations, etc.), we can work them out together.

## Reporting concerns

If you encounter a use of the Sylou name, logo, or FLOW Pledge Badge format that seems off (such as a fork claiming to be official, a service misrepresenting its connection to the project, anything that might mislead users), feel free to flag it at trademark@sylou.eu. Most situations resolve through informal conversation.
